Channels, invite links and public spaces

Written against @backendclaude's contract, which went live at 07:38 today. Nothing is built. This is for you to approve first.

One thing is wrong on the app right now

When you make a channel, our app still says this about Private:

Invite only. It stays hidden from the rest of the space.

That was true yesterday. Since 07:38 it is false. Private now means the name is shown and only the contents are shut. Hidden is the separate third one.

Our edit screen says the same thing, so there are two ways to hit it.

Not an emergency — you have said nobody is creating channels while this is in beta, so nobody has been misled. It is words, and it needs fixing before real people arrive.

Two more of the same kind

  • The space form says Open means anyone can find and join it. The space page calls that exact same setting “Open to the organization”. One setting, two meanings.
  • The old app's private switch never says what happens when you turn it off — which now makes the space public.

All three together: half a day. No backend needed. Ships on its own.

The order I would build it

1The three channel settings. A hidden channel must say “not found”, never “you cannot see this” — otherwise the message itself proves the channel is there. Half a day.

2The page somebody lands on when they tap an invite link. It has to work when they are signed out, and when they are a guest. The button says the right thing the first time, so nobody presses Join and then gets refused. About a day. This is the one that matters most.

3Making and managing links. Create one, see how many times it was used, kill it. Half a day.

4The two waiting lists. Approve or refuse. Where approving also lets somebody into a channel, the button has to say so — or an admin gives away access they never agreed to. About a day.

5Browse and join public spaces. Guests can read but not join or write, and we have to say why rather than just refuse. About a day.

One risk I want you to know about now

Step 2 has to hold on to the invite code while somebody signs up and confirms their email, then use it when they come back.

That means reading saved information on a fresh page load. That is exactly what broke onboarding in July, when people were bounced back to step one. I know the cause and I will build it the safe way.

I am telling you before I start because if I get it wrong, the person it strands is a brand new user who just tapped somebody's invite.
