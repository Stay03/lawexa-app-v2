Jurisdiction — the Madukolu test

What makes a court competent to hear a matter? I keep seeing Madukolu v Nkemdilim cited — what does it actually decide?
9:42 PM

Madukolu v Nkemdilim (1962) is the foundational authority on jurisdiction in Nigeria. The Federal Supreme Court, per Bairamian FJ, laid down the test that every court still applies before touching the merits of a case.

A court is competent only when all three conditions are met:

  1. Proper constitution — the bench is properly constituted as regards numbers and qualifications of its members, and no member is disqualified.
  2. Subject-matter jurisdiction — the case is within the court’s jurisdiction and no statutory limitation applies.
  3. Due process — the case comes before the court initiated by due process of law, and every condition precedent to exercising jurisdiction has been fulfilled.

Any defect in competence is fatal, for the proceedings are a nullity however well conducted and decided.

— Bairamian FJ
